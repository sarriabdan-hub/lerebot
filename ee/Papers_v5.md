## TIER 1 — Core: these papers set our decisions

### 1. π0: A Vision-Language-Action Flow Model for General Robot Control
**https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24164** · Physical Intelligence, 2024 · ~1.5h
The model family we started with: a VLM (PaliGemma) + an "action expert" that generates
50-step action chunks via flow matching.
**Focus on:** the architecture figure (VLM backbone vs action expert — this is what
`train_expert_only` freezes/trains); the action-chunking section (why the policy commits to
50 steps — the root of our n_action_steps and smoothness sagas); the data-mixture section
(why pretrained π0 knows general manipulation and fine-tuning only re-aims it).

### 2. π0.5: a VLA Model with Open-World Generalization
**https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16054** · Physical Intelligence, 2025 · ~1.5h
Our CURRENT model (ckpt_v4 = pi05 fine-tune). Same family as π0, trained for
generalization to unseen environments.
**Focus on:** what changed vs π0 (discrete-token "high-level" + continuous "low-level"
inference); the generalization experiments (unseen kitchens/homes) — this is the evidence
for why we bet on pi05 for the rack/camera-move goals; the co-training data recipe
(web data + robot data — why unfreezing vision must use a LOW learning rate, see #5).

### 3. Towards Accessible Physical AI: LoRA-Based Fine-Tuning of VLA Models for Real-World Robot Control
**https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11921** · 2025 · ~1h · **THE most decision-relevant paper for us**
LoRA fine-tuning of VLAs on an SO-101 arm — literally our hardware and our method space.
**Focus on:** (a) the **episode-count curve** — 20 eps = 18%, 50 ≈ 45–52%, 100 ≈ 68–72%,
200 ≈ 74–76% success — THIS is where the 30-eps-floor / 50-per-destination (600 total)
v5 budget comes from; (b) the **frozen vs unfrozen vision** comparison — 74% vs 76% at
200 eps (8.4M vs 33M trainable params, 12h vs 18h) — why unfreezing is a small win on
success rate but we want it anyway for camera-shift adaptation; (c) their LoRA config —
compare against why OUR LoRA run failed (see #11: scaling, target modules, LR).

### 4. Real-Time Execution of Action Chunking Flow Policies (RTC)
**https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07339** · Black, Galliker, Levine · NeurIPS 2025 · ~1h
The fix for the chunk-boundary problem: new chunks are generated by INPAINTING —
conditioning the flow model on the not-yet-executed tail of the previous chunk so the
handoff is continuous, executed asynchronously so there are no pauses.
**Focus on:** the problem statement (async seams vs sync pauses — we lived BOTH: our
naive async double-buffer jittered at every seam, sync missed grasps); the prefix-attention
/ guidance mechanism (what `prev_chunk_left_over` + `inference_delay` do in lerobot's
`predict_action_chunk`); the execution-horizon parameter (our `--rtc-execution-horizon`).
Context: our `--rtc` attempt used lerobot's port of exactly this paper; it lost to heavy
1-Euro in OUR stack partly because our jitter was dominated by the IK layer (see Papers
can't fix that — trace analysis in `vla-rtc-jitter-fix` memory).

### 5. Overcoming Catastrophic Forgetting in Neural Networks
**https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.00796** · Kirkpatrick et al., 2017 · ~45min (read selectively)
THE classic on why fine-tuning a pretrained network on narrow data destroys its general
abilities.
**Focus on:** just the intro + Fig 1 (the concept: new-task gradients overwrite weights
critical to old abilities). You don't need EWC's math. Practical takeaway for v5's
unfrozen-vision run: vision-encoder LR ~10× lower than the action expert's, early stopping
on val loss (#early-stopping in PLAN_FABLE §5), and don't train far past the val-loss knee —
that's the entire defense we'll deploy.

## TIER 2 — v5 robustness: racks, cameras, viewpoints

### 6. LIBERO-Plus: In-depth Robustness Analysis of VLA Models
**https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13626** · 2025 · ~1h
Systematically breaks VLA policies across 7 perturbation dimensions.
**Focus on:** the ranking of what hurts most — **camera viewpoint and robot initial state**
top the list (models drop from 95% to <30% under modest perturbation). This is the paper
behind two of our rules: (a) cameras stay rigidly mounted and aligned to reference frames,
(b) HOME_POSE must match the training start pose (our "flails 10s then works" bug was
exactly their "robot initial state" fragility). Also see the object-layout perturbation
results — the argument for rack-shifting in the v5 diversity checklist.

### 7. Do You Know Where Your Camera Is? View-Invariant Policy Learning with Camera Conditioning
**https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02268** · 2025 · ~45min
Makes policies viewpoint-robust by conditioning on camera extrinsics (Plücker ray embeddings).
**Focus on:** the "stair" data-collection scheme (overlapping camera poses across episodes) —
this is the recipe IF we ever do the multi-POV tier (PLAN_FABLE §3b); and the core insight
that a policy CAN handle a moving camera when it's told (or can infer) where the camera is —
why our wrist cam survives motion (pose derivable from joints) while a silently-moved top
cam kills placement.

### 8. VLA Models Are More Generalizable Than You Think: Revisiting Physical and Spatial Modeling
**https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02902** · 2025 · ~45min
Counterpoint to #6: the robustness is latent in pretrained VLAs and can be reactivated with
targeted, lightweight visual adaptation instead of massive re-collection.
**Focus on:** their "targeted adaptation" experiments — the theoretical backing for our
cheap camera-move recovery protocol (20–30 re-anchor episodes + warm-start finetune instead
of full re-collection), and for expecting the unfrozen-vision v5 run to adapt with modest data.

## TIER 3 — Phase 3: self-improvement + auto-assessment

### 9. Precise and Dexterous Robotic Manipulation via Human-in-the-Loop RL (HIL-SERL)
**https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21845** · 2024 · ~1h
Real-world RL that reaches near-perfect success in 1–2.5 hours of robot time, seeded by
demos + human corrections. Implemented in LeRobot (`hilserl`).
**Focus on:** the reward setup (binary success classifiers — exactly what LocateAnything
gives us for free); the human-correction mechanism (you nudge the arm when it drifts — the
"HIL" that replaces random exploration); and read honestly the RESET requirement between
episodes — our alternate-direction vial task (rack→rack forever) is the self-resetting
variant that makes this practical. This is the blueprint for PLAN_FABLE step 9.

### 10. LocateAnything-3B (model card)
**https://huggingface.co/nvidia/LocateAnything-3B** · NVIDIA, 2026 · ~20min
Open-vocabulary text→boxes localization VLM (Parallel Box Decoding, 3B params).
**Focus on:** the prompt format + output format (what `ee/assess.py` will call); throughput
notes (fits Thor alongside pi05). **⚠ LICENSE: academic/non-profit research ONLY —
commercial use requires NVIDIA approval. Since this is company work, check this with your
boss/legal BEFORE building it into the product; for internal eval-scoring experiments it's
likely fine, but flag it.**

### 15. Robometer: Scaling General-Purpose Robotic Reward Models via Trajectory Comparisons
**https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02115** · RSS 2026, USC/UT-Dallas/MIT/UW/NVIDIA · ~1h ·
site: https://robometer.github.io/ · code: https://github.com/robometer/robometer
Video+instruction → per-frame progress + success prediction, zero-shot across embodiments
(Qwen3-VL-4B; 1M+ trajectories, 21 embodiments).
**Focus on:** the failure-detection section (temporal reward inconsistencies, F1 0.81 — our
future task-level watchdog); the DSRL real-robot result (π0 20%→85% with Robometer as the
automatic success signal — the phase-3 RL blueprint); and the RBM-EVAL-OOD table — **SO-101
is in their OOD eval**, so zero-shot on our arm class is tested, not hoped. Candidate to
replace LocateAnything as our success judge (PLAN_FABLE §4b); validate on our recorded
videos FIRST — that pilot runs on the WS while the arm is down.

### 16. GR00T N1 / N1.5 — the hedge model (NVIDIA)
**Paper (N1):** https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14734 · **N1.5 tech report:**
https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/gr00t-n1_5/ · **SO-101 post-training guide:**
https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/gr00t-n1-5-so101-tuning ·
**LeRobot integration:** https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/groot ·
**Thor deploy walkthrough:** https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/fine_tune_gr00t_n1.5_for_lerobot_so_arm_and_deploy_on_jetson_thor/ · ~1.5h total
NVIDIA's open VLA family (Eagle VLM + diffusion-transformer action head, 16-step chunks).
**Focus on:** the **EmbodimentTag / new-embodiment post-training** section of the N1 paper
(that's the mechanism the SO-101 guide uses); the N1.5 changes (frozen VLM, better
language-following vs N1); and the SO-101 blog's dataset/config walkthrough — it consumes
LeRobot datasets directly, so OUR v5 data trains it with no conversion. Why it's hedge #1
(PLAN_FABLE §7): best-supported model on our exact hardware pair (SO-101 + Thor), already
`policy.type=groot` in our lerobot. Why it's NOT primary: pi0.5 beats it decisively on
language-conditioned success (0.67 vs 0.40 — VILAS benchmark, arXiv 2605.02037), and
prompt-controlled destinations are our core requirement. Note if we ever run it: 16-step
chunks ≈ our n_action_steps=15 regime natively, but our RTC machinery is pi05-specific.

## TIER 4 — Foundations: why things work (and why ours failed)

### 11. LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models
**https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685** · Hu et al., Microsoft, 2021 · ~1h
The original adapter method: freeze the base model, train small low-rank matrices.
**Focus on:** the **α/r scaling factor** (sec 4.1) — LeRobot's integration effectively gave
our run 0.5× scaling → adapters too weak — THE bug behind "LoRA didn't work for some
reason"; the target-modules choice (attention-only vs +MLP — ours missed the MLPs); and the
guidance that adapter training wants a HIGHER learning rate than full fine-tuning (ours was
too low). Read this and our failed run becomes obvious in hindsight.

### 12. Flow Matching for Generative Modeling
**https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747** · Lipman et al., 2022 · ~1.5h (math-heavy, skim OK)
The generative method inside π0/π0.5's action expert.
**Focus on:** just the concept: the model learns a velocity field that transports noise →
data in a few integration steps. Practical consequences you've already observed: actions
are **sampled** (stochastic — why two identical executes differ), each chunk needs ~10
denoising steps (why inference takes ~0.5s — the latency the async/RTC machinery hides),
and RTC's inpainting works by guiding this exact denoising process. Skip the theorems.

### 13. Learning Fine-Grained Bimanual Manipulation with Low-Cost Hardware (ACT / ALOHA)
**https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705** · Zhao et al., 2023 · ~1h
Where **action chunking** comes from: predict a sequence of actions, execute, re-plan.
**Focus on:** the action-chunking motivation (compounding errors in single-step policies)
and temporal ensembling (their answer to chunk-boundary smoothness — compare with RTC #4);
the "why 50 steps" tradeoff — the origin of our n_action_steps=15-vs-50 experiments
(short = closed-loop grasp correction, long = smooth but blind).

### 14. 1€ Filter: A Simple Speed-based Low-pass Filter for Noisy Input
**https://gery.casiez.net/1euro/** · Casiez et al., CHI 2012 · ~30min
The adaptive filter running on your arm RIGHT NOW (`--filter oneeuro`, min_cutoff 0.3,
beta 0.02).
**Focus on:** the two-knob intuition — `min_cutoff` = smoothing at rest (lower = smoother,
laggier), `beta` = how fast smoothing releases during quick motion (higher = more
responsive). The interactive demo on that page lets you FEEL the tradeoff — 10 minutes
there and you'll tune the arm better than any of our sweep scripts.
